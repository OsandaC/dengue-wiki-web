# Racoubian2016 - ANA Prevalence in Healthy Lebanese Subjects

**Full citation:** Racoubian E, Zubaid RM, Shareef MA, Almawi WY. Prevalence of antinuclear antibodies in healthy Lebanese subjects, 2008–2015: a cross-sectional study involving 10,814 subjects. *Rheumatology International* 2016. DOI: 10.1007/s00296-016-3533-0.

**Raw file:** `[[raw/racoubian2016.pdf]]`

## Summary

This is a large cross-sectional study (2008–2015) of antinuclear antibody (ANA) prevalence in a healthy Lebanese population, conducted at a single diagnostics centre in Beirut. Of 11,521 initially screened individuals (blood donors, visa applicants, pre-employment checkups), 10,814 were analysed after technical exclusions. ANA was tested by indirect immunofluorescence (IIF) on HEp-2 cells (Euroimmun AG). Overall ANA positivity at ≥1:100 was **26.4%** (2,860/10,814), with 696 (6.4%) exceeding 1:100. The study confirms the now-familiar pattern from other healthy-population ANA studies in this wiki — strong female predominance (1.5–2.5-fold) and a steady increase with age from the 30–40-year group through >70 years — and is explicitly framed by the authors as the **first study to examine ANA prevalence in a healthy Lebanese population**.

Beyond its own data, the paper's Table 1 assembles a multi-country comparison of healthy-population ANA prevalence (Lebanon, Australia, Belgium, Brazil, China, India, Italy, Japan, and three USA cohorts), which substantially expands the set of international population baselines available in this wiki for calibrating "elevated" vs. "background" ANA rates in dengue and other infection contexts.

This is **not a dengue paper** — like [[Tan1997 - ANA Range in Healthy Individuals]], [[Satoh2012 - ANA Prevalence in United States]], [[Li2019 - ANA Epidemiology in Chinese Healthy Population]], and [[Dinse2022 - Increasing ANA Prevalence in United States]], it is a general ANA-epidemiology reference that informs the [[Antinuclear Antibodies]] concept page and the wiki's interpretation of healthy-control ANA rates.

## Study Design
- **Type:** Cross-sectional, retrospective review of clinical laboratory records (2008–2015)
- **Sample size:** 10,814 analysed (of 11,521 initially screened; 707 [6.1%] excluded for technical problems or sample volume). 3,311 males (mean age 31.8 ± 14.4 y) and 7,503 females (mean age 37.4 ± 13.0 y).
- **Setting:** St. Marc Medical and Diagnostic Center, East Beirut, Lebanon — an integrated clinical diagnostics centre, 2008–2015
- **Population:** Self-reported asymptomatic individuals (blood donors, foreign visa applicants, pre-employment checkups) from the five major Lebanese provinces and the two major religious groups. Excluded if overt autoimmune disease, conditions linked to abnormal ANA titres (infection, malignancy), or use of drugs known to alter ANA (procainamide, chlorpromazine, isoniazid, methyldopa, quinidine, sulfasalazine, sulfonamides). Minors (<18 y) included with guardian consent.

## Key Findings

- **Overall ANA positivity (≥1:100): 26.4%** (2,860/10,814); of these, 696 (6.4% of the total cohort) had titres exceeding 1:100.
- **Titre distribution (% of total cohort, P<0.005):** 20.0% at 1:100, 3.7% at 1:320, 1.7% at 1:640, 1.1% at ≥1:1000 — i.e. 75.7% (2,164/2,860) of all ANA-positive cases were at the lowest titre (1:100), and 10.4% (297/2,860) exceeded 1:640.
- **Female predominance:** ANA positivity consistently higher in females across all years, ages, and titres (P<0.005 / P=0.001); female:male ratio ranged from **1.54-fold (2013) to 2.40-fold (2008)**, with an overall range of **1.5- to 2.5-fold**.
- **Gender-specific titre breakdown (Fig. 5):** at 1:100, males 14.6% vs. females 22.4% (total 20.0%); at 1:320, 2.1% vs. 4.4% (total 3.7%); at 1:640, 0.6% vs. 2.1% (total 1.7%); at ≥1:1000, 0.3% vs. 1.4% (total 1.1%).
- **Age association:** steady, statistically significant increase in ANA positivity from the 30–40-year age group through the >70-year group (P=0.001), consistently higher in females at every age band.
- **Age × titre interaction:** 45% of *low*-titre positives (1:100–1:320) occurred in the 31–60-year group vs. 19.8% in the 61+ group (P<0.001); by contrast, *high*-titre positives (>1:640) were distributed more uniformly across 31+ age groups (11.4% at 31–40 y to 12.4% at >70 y).
- **Temporal trend 2008–2015:** ANA positivity rose progressively, with most positive cases recorded 2013–2015. The authors attribute this primarily to an improvement in **reading method and reporting training introduced in 2010** (same microscope, IF kits, and manufacturer — Euroimmune — throughout the study period), not to a change in assay or to environmental exposure.

*Table 1 lists "10,851" for Lebanon, matching the abstract's "study subjects comprised 10,851 individuals"; the Results section and title both state 10,814 (= 11,521 − 707 exclusions). This ~37-subject discrepancy appears to be an internal inconsistency in the paper (see Relevance & Notes) and does not materially affect the 26.4% headline (2860/10,851 = 26.35%; 2860/10,814 = 26.45%).

**A second, more consequential internal inconsistency: Japan's value.** In the Discussion, the authors write that Lebanon's 26.4% "was reminiscent of a US study (25%; [6]), Belgian (23.5%; [9]), and Australian (28%; [11]) studies... in sharp contrast to the very low rates reported for Chinese (5.92%; [16]), **Japanese (9.5%; [17])**, and Indians (12.3%; [18])." But Table 1 lists **Japan at 25.9%**, citing the *same* reference [17] (Hayashi et al.) used for the Discussion's "9.5%" figure. The Discussion's own narrative groups Japan with the *low* cluster (China/Japan/India, 5.9–12.3%) rather than the *high* cluster (US/Belgium/Australia, 23.5–28%) where Table 1's 25.9% would place it — these two framings of Hayashi et al.'s data are mutually exclusive, and the paper does not reconcile them. **Effect on this wiki's use of Table 1:** the "high-20s cluster" (Lebanon 26.4%, Australia 28.3%, USA Dallas 25.2%, USA African-American 30.8%) does not depend on Japan's value either way, so the core international-variability argument is unaffected — but Japan's 25.9% (used in [[Antinuclear Antibodies]] §International Variability) should be treated as the *less-supported* of the two figures Racoubian2016 itself reports for Hayashi et al., not as an independently verified datum.

- The authors explicitly state: **"This is the first study that examines the prevalence of ANA in healthy Lebanese subjects."**

## Methods Used
- [[Indirect Immunofluorescence ANA Test]] — HEp-2 cell substrate, Euroimmun AG kit per manufacturer's instructions. Positivity reported at "≥1:100" (Abstract/Results), though the Methods section states ANA positivity was defined as ">1:100" — the data (20.0% specifically *at* 1:100, summing into the 26.4% total) confirm that 1:100 itself was counted as positive, so "≥1:100" is the operative threshold despite the Methods wording. Titre bands: low 1:100–1:320, moderate 1:320–1:640, high >1:640. Dual-reader blinded scoring of digital images, with 97.5% inter-reader agreement and third-reviewer adjudication (plus repeat staining) for the remaining 2.5%.

## Relevance & Notes

This paper directly addresses a gap flagged in `state.md` on 2026-06-13: the wiki's primary thread had identified "Lebanon (PubMed 27432022, n=10,814, 26.4% at ≥1:100)" as a candidate high-baseline international precedent, but only from an unverified search snippet. This ingest provides the full, verified dataset.

**Widening the international baseline comparator.** [[ANA and Dengue - Review V3.0]] (§Part II) currently frames the ~13–16% figures from Satoh2012/Dinse2022/Li2019 (all 1:80 or >1:100) as "the" healthy-population baseline against which dengue-associated ANA elevations (Chatterjee2024, Gawali2021, Garcia2009) are judged. Racoubian2016 complicates that framing substantially: at a **≥1:100 cutoff**, healthy Lebanon sits at **26.4%** — well above the ~13–16% range. Table 1 shows Lebanon is not an outlier either: Australia (28.3%), USA African-American (30.8%), Japan (25.9%), and USA Dallas (25.2%) are all comparably high, while China (5.92%) and India North (12.3%) are far lower. **Healthy-population ANA prevalence varies by roughly 5-fold (5.9–30.8%) across ethnic/geographic groups even before considering cutoff differences.** This means "the healthy population ANA rate" used as a comparator for dengue-associated ANA elevations should be understood as one point on a much wider international range, not a fixed reference value.

**Cutoff-matched comparison is still imprecise.** The closest cutoff match in the wiki is [[Li2019 - ANA Epidemiology in Chinese Healthy Population]] (>1:100, 14.01% in a Chinese health-checkup population) — Lebanon's 26.4% at the same nominal cutoff is nearly double. This is itself a striking ethnic/geographic difference at a matched threshold, independent of any dengue-specific question, and underscores that "the healthy population ANA rate" is not a single number.

**Table 1's own cutoffs are not uniform — verified from the discussion text.** Table 1 carries no cutoff column, but the Discussion states that Wang et al.'s China figure (5.92%) was reported at a *stricter* >1:160 cutoff — "more recent studies adopted higher cutoff (>1:160) values [13]," vs. earlier studies' 1:40/1:160 (ref. [30]) and Racoubian2016's own ≥1:100. No cutoff is stated for the other 9 comparator rows. Interestingly, this is roughly consistent with [[Li2019 - ANA Epidemiology in Chinese Healthy Population]]'s own within-cohort cutoff gradient (>1:100 → 14.01%; >1:320 → 5.93%) — a >1:160-class cutoff landing China's Table 1 figure near 5.9% is plausible on that basis, suggesting at least *part* of China's outlier-low position is a cutoff artefact rather than a purely ethnic/geographic one. The high end of the range (Lebanon, Australia, Japan, USA Dallas, USA African-American, all ~25–31%) has no stated cutoffs at all and should not be assumed cutoff-matched to Lebanon's ≥1:100 either.

**Why this is not a dengue-thread paper but still belongs in the wiki:** Lebanon is not a dengue-endemic country and this study has no infection, autoimmune-disease, or dengue content. Its sole function in this wiki is to **stabilise the population-baseline reference set** against which dengue-associated ANA elevations (Chatterjee2024, Gawali2021, Garcia2009) are judged — population-baseline data in the wiki was previously thin.

**Geography:** Lebanon is a new country in this wiki (1 source). Per the ≥2-source rule, no standalone geography page is created; recorded here and flagged in the index/Watch Items.

**Limitations (author-stated):** cross-sectional design — cannot estimate the proportion of ANA-positive individuals who will develop future autoimmune disease; could not assess modifying factors (infections, subclinical autoimmunity, drug exposure) on the autoantibody profile; ethnic diversity of the Lebanese population not considered in the analysis; "healthy" status is self-reported (no independent verification beyond the stated exclusion criteria).

## Questions Raised

- The paper reports only a ≥1:100 cutoff. What would the Lebanese healthy-population rate be at the 1:80 EULAR/ACR standard cutoff used by Satoh2012 and Dinse2022? Given that lower cutoffs increase positivity, 26.4% at ≥1:100 implies the 1:80 rate would be higher still — potentially among the highest healthy-population rates in this wiki.
- The authors attribute the 2008–2015 temporal increase primarily to a 2010 reading/training upgrade (an assay-reading artefact), in contrast to [[Dinse2022 - Increasing ANA Prevalence in United States]], which argues a genuine secular increase (not explained by methodological drift) occurred in the US over a similar period using a single-lab, single-kit design. Could a genuine environmental/secular component also be present in the Lebanese trend, masked by the reading-quality confound the authors describe?
- No infection, autoimmune-disease, or dengue data are present in this study. Could a Lebanese or broader Middle Eastern dengue cohort (Lebanon has had sporadic imported dengue) ever be ingested to test whether this unusually high healthy baseline (26.4–28%+ range alongside Australia) interacts with infection-triggered ANA in a dengue-endemic setting?
